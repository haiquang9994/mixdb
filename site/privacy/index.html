Privacy Policy

For the MixDB desktop application · Effective 31 August 2026 · Last updated 31 August 2026

MixDB collects nothing about you. There is no account to create, no server operated by us that the app talks to, and no analytics, telemetry, crash reporting or advertising code anywhere in it.

Your connections, credentials and queries stay on your computer. The rest of this page explains precisely where they are kept and the only two occasions on which MixDB contacts the internet by itself.

Questions about this policy, or about privacy in MixDB generally, go to haiquang9994@outlook.com.

2. Personal data we collect

None. We do not collect, receive, transmit, store, process or have access to any personal data from users of MixDB. Specifically, MixDB contains:

  • no account system, sign-in or user identifier;
  • no usage analytics, product telemetry or event tracking;
  • no crash or error reporting service;
  • no advertising, advertising identifiers or marketing trackers;
  • no third-party analytics or data-collection SDKs of any kind.

Because we receive nothing, we have nothing to sell, share, disclose or hand over. We do not sell or share personal information, and we never have.

3. What MixDB stores on your device

MixDB is a working tool, so it remembers your work. All of it is written to your own computer and none of it is sent anywhere.

3.1 Configuration files

Saved database connections, saved SSH hosts, REST requests and environments, query drafts and history, and your app settings are stored as plain JSON files in the application data folder:

Windows
%APPDATA%\io.github.haiquang9994.mixdb
macOS
~/Library/Application Support/io.github.haiquang9994.mixdb
Linux
~/.config/io.github.haiquang9994.mixdb

3.2 Passwords and secrets

Database passwords, SSH credentials and any REST environment value you mark as secret are not written to those files. They are handed to the credential store your operating system already provides, under the name MixDB:

  • Windows — Windows Credential Manager
  • macOS — Keychain
  • Linux — Secret Service (GNOME Keyring, KWallet and compatible)

MixDB reads a secret back only when a connection you started needs it. It is never logged, and debug output prints such values as "***".

3.3 Interface state

Your theme, accent colour, interface language, open tabs and which update you chose to skip are kept in the application's local storage on your device.

4. When MixDB contacts the internet on its own

Exactly twice. Everywhere else, MixDB is silent unless you tell it to connect somewhere.

4.1 Checking for updates

A few seconds after launch, and when you press the check button in Settings, MixDB requests the release manifest published at github.com and compares its version against the running one. It sends no identifier and no information about you or your work; GitHub, like any web server, sees the IP address and standard user agent of the request. That data is handled under GitHub's Privacy Statement. We receive nothing from it and keep no record of it.

This check is currently part of how the app starts and cannot be switched off from Settings. If you would rather it never ran, block the application's network access at your firewall — MixDB continues to work normally; only the update notice stops arriving.

4.2 Downloading database command-line tools

Dump and restore need the vendors' own tools. When — and only when — you ask MixDB to install them, it downloads them from the vendor:

  • mysqldump / mysql from dev.mysql.com (Oracle)
  • pg_dump / psql from get.enterprisedb.com (EDB)
  • mongodump / mongorestore from downloads.mongodb.org and fastdl.mongodb.org (MongoDB, Inc.)

Those sites see your IP address and user agent as they would for any download. Each has its own privacy policy. Nothing about you or your databases is sent with the request, and none of it happens until you press the button.

5. Connections you make yourself

Database connections, SSH tunnels and REST requests travel directly from your computer to the address you entered. There is no proxy, relay, cloud sync or intermediary of ours in the path — we could not see that traffic even if we wanted to.

What those servers do with the requests they receive is governed by whoever operates them, not by this policy. If you connect to a third party's database or API, their terms and privacy practices apply to that exchange.

6. Children

MixDB is a technical tool for software developers and database administrators. It is not directed at children, and we do not knowingly collect personal data from anyone — children included — because we do not collect personal data at all.

7. Your rights and how to exercise them

Data-protection laws such as the GDPR and the CCPA give you rights of access, correction, deletion, portability and objection against whoever holds your data. We hold none, so there is no request for us to fulfil and no profile of you for us to produce. We are not a controller or processor of your personal data.

Everything MixDB remembers is on your machine and under your direct control:

  • See or export it — the configuration files are readable JSON; open or copy them at any time.
  • Delete it — delete the application data folder listed in section 3.1, then remove the entries named MixDB from your operating system's credential store. Uninstalling the app does not remove either automatically.

If anything here is unclear, write to haiquang9994@outlook.com and we will answer.

8. Security

Secrets are delegated to the operating system's credential store rather than kept in files of our own, so they are protected by the same mechanism that guards the rest of your machine's passwords. Every update is cryptographically signed and its signature is verified before a byte of it is executed.

No software is free of risk. If you believe you have found a security or privacy problem in MixDB, please report it to haiquang9994@outlook.com rather than posting it publicly.

9. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the revised version appears on this page with a new effective date. Changes that affect what MixDB does with your data are also called out in the release notes of the version that introduces them, so an update never quietly changes the terms you accepted.
